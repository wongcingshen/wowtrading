Auction Ledger

How to read a call

Each call says what to do, how many units the market can absorb, and what the price has to do for it to pay.

Strength
Lean, solid or strong. It comes from a score between −100 and +100. Strong calls (60 or more) are the ones emailed to you.
Price
The average price of the cheapest 15% of units on sale. One mispriced listing does not move it.
Normal price
The item's own average over the last 72 hours.
After the cut
Your gain if you buy now and sell at the normal price, once the auction house has taken its 5%.
Buy up to
The smaller of two numbers: units listed within 10% of the cheapest, and a quarter of a day's estimated sales. Past that, you are the market.
Days of cover
Units on sale divided by units sold per day. Under one day, the shelf is nearly empty.
24 hour forecast
What the learning model expects from buying now and selling a day later, after the cut. Compare it with the item's usual daily swing: a forecast smaller than the swing is noise.
Half-life
How many hours this item has usually taken to close half of a gap to its normal price.

Why a call appears

Dip
Price far below normal while supply is steady.
Squeeze
Units on sale far below normal while price climbs.
Spike
Price far above normal. A reason to sell what you hold.
Flood
Listings piling up while price falls. A reason to stay out.
Cheap vs similar items
Unusually cheap next to items that do the same job.
Event
A scheduled event that lifts demand is coming.

Sales are estimated from listings that vanish between hourly snapshots, so they run low. Prices come from Blizzard's Game Data API. Not affiliated with Blizzard.
